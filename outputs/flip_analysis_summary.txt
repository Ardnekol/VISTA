Key evidence from the report
----------------------------
1) Modifier-level differences: Analysis 1 reports substantial variation in flip rates by axis (authority signal 3.3%, self-preservation 2.8%, time pressure 2.3% vs resource scarcity 0.9%). If flips were driven solely by scenario or profile, we would expect more uniformity across modifier axes.

2) Axis × Profile interactions: Analysis 5 shows that different axes flip at different rates depending on profile strength. For weak profiles (0–1 HIGH), Social Visibility and Time Pressure produce high flip rates (8.3% each). For medium profiles, Authority Signal dominates (5.8%). For strong profiles (4+ HIGH), Self-Preservation and Time Pressure still appear but at much lower absolute rates. These interactions indicate a three-way dependence: modifier × profile × scenario context.

3) Scenario heterogeneity: Analysis 3 shows some scenarios (e.g., SC002_2 at 5.7%) have much higher flip rates than others (several at 0%). The presence of scenarios with both high and low flip rates indicates scenario matters; but modifiers remain predictive within profile groups (see Analysis 5), so scenario alone doesn't explain flips.

4) Profile strength gradients: Analysis 2 demonstrates that profiles with few or no HIGH values flip more often (e.g., 0–1 HIGH at 5.0–6.5%) than profiles with strong HIGH counts (≥4 HIGH at ~1.0–2.2%). This supports the idea that profile strength moderates susceptibility to modifiers.

5) Reliability and triangulation: Dot-product flips (19.9%) and LLM flips (2.0%) differ in magnitude; however, the axis and profile trends are visible in the LLM results. Disagreement metrics (Analysis 4) indicate nontrivial differences between methods but not enough to invalidate the observed patterns.

Quantitative interpretation
---------------------------
- The modifier axis effect size: the highest axis (Authority Signal, 3.3%) has ~3.7× the flip rate of the lowest (Resource Scarcity, 0.9%). That's a meaningful difference for behavioral data.
- Profile moderation: Weak profiles flip roughly 3–4× more often than strong profiles in absolute terms (compare 5–6% vs ~1–2%).
- Combined evidence points to modifiers exerting independent effects beyond scenario and profile. The Axis × Profile table directly shows which modifiers are most potent for which profile strengths.
